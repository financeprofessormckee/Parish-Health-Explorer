    About the numbers

    Everything here is extracted from the Archdiocese of Detroit's own parish restructuring workbooks — one public PDF per parish. Nothing is estimated, modelled or supplied from another source. Figures are fiscal year 2024/25 unless labelled otherwise.

    What the measures mean

    Net surplus / deficit
    Revenue minus expenses for the fiscal year, as reported to the archdiocese.
    Months of runway
    Unrestricted cash and savings divided by one month of the parish's own spending. It answers the question a bare deficit can't: how long could this parish keep going without changing anything? Restricted savings and endowments are excluded, because the parish cannot freely spend them.
    Seating utilization
    Mass attendance as a share of seating capacity per Mass, as the workbooks report it.
    Funerals per baptism
    A parish burying more people than it baptizes is shrinking from the inside, regardless of its balance sheet.
    Deferred maintenance
    The total estimated cost of repairs identified in the parish's property condition evaluation. Blank means no evaluation has been completed — which is not the same as no repairs needed.
    Decade change
    Percent change 2015–2024, as printed in the workbook. Blank where the 2015 figure was zero and the change is undefined.

    The at-risk flags

    Five yes/no signals of distress, counted rather than weighted so the result is always explainable: the parish ran a deficit; its three-year net trend is worsening; it held more funerals than baptisms; it fills fewer than 30% of its seats; and it holds under five years (60 months) of cash.

    This is a distress ranking, not a prediction. It measures what the published numbers say about a parish's position today. It does not know anything about the archdiocese's intentions, a parish's plans, its people, or its role in its neighbourhood — and no parish should be read as "closing" because it scores highly here.

    Caveats

    • Parish names in the archdiocese's comparison tables differ slightly from its own listing page (one calls it "Blessed Sacrament Cathedral", the other "Cathedral of the Most Blessed Sacrament"). Records are matched on planning area plus name; a handful of near-identical names may be imperfectly joined.
    • Demographic figures (median income, share of Catholic households, median age) are reported for the whole planning area, not the individual parish.
    • A blank is a blank: where the workbook printed nothing, "N/A" or "--", nothing is shown here. Blanks are never treated as zero.
